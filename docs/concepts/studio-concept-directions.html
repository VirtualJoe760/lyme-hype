Lyme Hype Studio — internal review

Two ways into the room

Same app, same job — a canvas-driven studio for turning a track into keyframes, motion graphics, and Seedance video, then handing the cut to Premiere. Generated nodes live and combine right on the canvas (the stephenlawyer.clothing pattern) — every node can be generated, uploaded, or pulled in from a web link, and video/audio nodes send straight to the timeline. The left rail is now Sessions, nameable like Claude Desktop's chat list. Storyboard sits alongside the freeform canvas for blocking out prompts before spending a real generation, and Play is its own full-width destination — not another canvas toggle — for reviewing and cutting a single video or audio node, with a back arrow returning to whichever view sent you there. Generation tools and data tools like ChatRealty attach as MCP connections rather than hardcoded integrations — see the Connections panel below. Both side panels collapse out of the way, and what differs below is just the visual system wrapped around it.

  • Not a canvas overlay anymore — Play takes the whole body. Sessions and the Add-to-canvas rail step aside rather than sharing the room, so the video itself gets real size instead of fighting for space.
  • Controls are overlaid on the video, not a separate row underneath it — play/pause, the time readouts, and the trim handles all live in one slim bar at the bottom edge of the frame, the same place a normal video player puts them.
  • The audio strip is one thin bar, not a second boxed component that mirrors the video — a compact waveform with its own trim handles, sized to be easy to grab, not to look important.
  • Detach pulls the audio out as its own independent node on Canvas; ✕ deletes it from the clip outright. Both act on the audio track specifically — they don't touch the video.
  • The back arrow replaces the breadcrumb while in Play and returns to whichever view sent you here — Canvas or Storyboard — not always Canvas by default.
  • Trimming is still non-destructive — in/out points live on the node's own data, not a cut of the underlying generated file. Reopening Play later shows the full original with the last trim remembered.
  • Split at playhead turns one clip into two nodes back on Canvas — the way to pull three good seconds out of a longer generation without regenerating it.

Save
  • "Custom" is the default tab, not a fallback after three pre-built rows — the form is the same whether you pick a quick-start template or type in something we've never heard of. Templates just pre-fill it.
  • "Help me set this up" is the agent-copilot entry point — it drives a real browser to the service's signup or API-keys page and narrates, but never types into the credential field itself.
  • The secure entry modal is a separate, isolated piece — not a field inside the connection form. That's the boundary: the agent can get the user to this screen, but everything typed here goes straight to OS-native storage, reported back only as field name, length, and last 4 characters.
  • Same MCP client model Claude Desktop and Claude Code already use — the agent gets a tool's capabilities the moment it's connected, no per-provider plumbing on our end.
  • ChatRealty specifically is what makes "listing video" a real feature: search a listing, pull its real photos, and hand them straight to the canvas as image nodes — no manual download-and-drag.
